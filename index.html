BartonI design hardware, then build it, then break it on purpose.

I am a junior mechanical engineer at Purdue and I am aiming at research and development, because R&D is the only job description that includes all the parts I like. I do not want to hand a model over a wall. I want to draw the part, run the analysis, program the toolpath, cut it, solder the board that drives it, write the firmware, take it somewhere rough, and then find out what I got wrong.

Right now that looks like a survey rover in its third generation for a wildfire-risk company, a balancing biped on my bench with its head open, and a 60-person robotics team whose budget and shop I run. The management minor is there for the same reason as the machine shop hours: a design that nobody can afford to build or sell is not finished.
