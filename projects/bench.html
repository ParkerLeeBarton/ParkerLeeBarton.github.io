01Purdue ME hammer

Every Purdue mechanical engineering student machines their own hammer. It is a rite of passage and most people stop at a hammer. I used mine as an anodising test piece, applying everything I had learned about surface prep, current control and process consistency on the putter heads.

After a lot of trial and error on earlier parts, this was the first time the whole process went right first pass, which was a good indication that I had actually learned something rather than got lucky once.

02Dorm hardware: the one that became a business

Freshman year I started making clip-on accessories for standard dorm furniture: lofted beds, desks, shelves. It began as hooks for my own room and turned into a proper design exercise: getting a snap fit that holds reliably without marking or damaging university furniture means tuning clearance and print orientation together, and I went through a lot of iterations before the parts stopped cracking at the hook root.

Then people started asking for them. I printed in batches in my dorm and sold a lot of the furniture-mounted ones around campus.

This is the project that made me add the management minor. Designing something that works is one problem. Designing something a stranger will hand you money for, in a form you can produce repeatedly, is a different one, and I liked it just as much.

Why the minor exists

03Parametric robotic hand

A fully parametric robotic hand modelled entirely in CAD. The whole model is driven by a handful of key measurements, fingertip size being the main one, and everything else scales from them. I used configurations to cut down part files and keep the assembly manageable.

It is fully jointed and animated with a string-pull tendon system that could be driven by servos. The point was flexibility, both in motion and in how easily the whole design rescales. It is not built yet; it may become the hand on the balancing robot.

04Family crest

My dad found a century-old document with our family crest on it and asked whether I could bring it back. The original was faded and corroded, so I researched historical references for the symbols and conventions, then redrew the crest digitally in CAD, keeping the elements and period styling intact rather than modernising them.

From there I laser cut it into keychains and small pieces as gifts for the family. Research, CAD and fabrication in service of turning a fragile artifact into something people can carry.

05Electronics clock

Part of the ME curriculum: build a digital clock from a supplied PCB and a handful of components. I soldered and assembled the board, then went past the assignment and designed a printed case with a thin polycarbonate front cover.

It is a simple thing and I use it every day, which is a better outcome than most coursework gets.

06The rest

Three-hour Bluetooth RC car

A self-imposed challenge: CAD, print, wire, solder and programme a Bluetooth-controlled car in three hours including print time. ESP32-C3 Mini, a couple of transistors, two DC motors, programmed through the Arduino IDE.

It worked badly. That was fine: the constraint was time, not quality, and it is where I learned the ESP32 toolchain that the MySwing grip later ran on.

Wood bench

Built with my roommate as dorm furniture. Wooden frame, TPU feet I designed so it does not scrape the floor when it moves, and a cushion made from leftover fabric off the backpack. Not complicated, but a good example of making something instead of buying it.

Cat tower

My roommates and I designed and built a multi-level cat tower after one of us acquired a cat. Modelled in CAD first to plan dimensions and structure, then cut and assembled with hand tools. The frame takes a replaceable scratch pad, which is the only part of a cat tower that ever wears out.

Tracked skateboard

After my bike was stolen I switched to a skateboard, then designed a riser that mounts between the deck and the trucks with a hidden cavity sized for a tracker. Printed in carbon fibre filled filament. The custom-cut grip tape underneath it has no performance justification whatsoever.
